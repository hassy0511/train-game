# Stylized character modeling rules

- Primitive shapes are blockout tools, not a default final construction.
- Low poly means intentional planes and transitions, not merely a small polygon count.
- Design cheek, forehead, muzzle, chest, abdomen, thigh, and clothing planes deliberately.
- Build width, height, and depth together; matching the front view alone is insufficient.
- Interpret graphic eyes as belonging to the face surface unless the reference explicitly shows spherical volume.
- Do not invent realistic eye sockets, nasal bridges, lips, jaws, or anatomy absent from the approved design.
- Treat hat, hair, ears, tail, cape, and coat outline as primary silhouette forms.
- Avoid over-smoothing into balloon shapes. Preserve the approved angularity and controlled low-poly facets.
- Hands and feet need designed silhouettes; do not leave accidental sphere or capsule ends.
- Resolve visible gaps and intersections before materials and beauty lighting.
